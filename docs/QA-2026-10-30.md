# BeyondBinary end-to-end QA — 30 October 2026

This is the baseline test report. See [the subsequent fixes and remaining limits](QA-FIXES-2026-10-30.md)
for the current implementation and verification results.

## Decision

The live app is operational and GPU inference is substantially faster. It is
**not ready to be described as fully correct or fully compliant with all three
judging challenges**. The most urgent issues concern source identity, unsupported
verdicts, missing decision traces, and explanations that overstate what was checked.

This was a testing pass. Application behavior was not changed during it. The earlier
GPU and comparison-request fixes remained active. No deployment, model retraining,
index rebuild, external publication, or feedback submission was performed.

## Findings requiring fixes

P1 means fix before relying on the result in the evaluation or broad public use.
P2 means functional/interpretation defect or important limitation. P3 is maintenance
or presentation. Priorities describe this demonstration's needs, not a formal CVSS score.

### QA-01 · P1 · A requested article can silently become another publisher's article

**Reproduction:** From the politics wire, audit the NYT story titled “As Midterm Ads
Focus on Epstein, a Florida Senator Is the Latest Target.” Its requested URL ends
`/2026/10/29/us/politics/epstein-victim-ashley-moody-ad.html`.

**Observed:** The fetched headline/body became CNN's “Epstein floods the midterms as
campaigns spend $35 million on more than 160 ads.” The URL field and subsequent
AllSides prior still identified the original NYT article. Thus claims from one
article were assessed with another publisher's identity.

**Cause:** `backend/article_fetcher.py:190` searches broadly and accepts another
candidate's body or assembled snippets. `Workbench.tsx:67` uses returned text/title
but does not adopt returned URL; analysis then receives the original URL.

**Required:** Preserve requested and actual source separately; only ingest the intended
article or an explicitly identified alternate. Fail clearly when it cannot be fetched.
Do not blend search snippets from several stories into a purported original article.

Evidence: `ui-protected-url-replaced-article.txt`, `ui-news-article-audited.txt`.

### QA-02 · P1 · Irrelevant evidence can produce a confident factual verdict

**Input:** An unpublished private experiment conducted yesterday by the fictional
Zorvax Laboratory measured exactly 713 invisible particles.

**Observed:** Refuted, confidence 0.762, score zero. Retrieved evidence concerned the
Philadelphia Experiment and dark matter; it did not investigate the stated laboratory
or event. This is an abstention/relevance failure, regardless of the fabricated input.

**Cause:** `backend/verifier.py:149` accepts available search passages without a strong
claim-specific relevance gate before NLI. Retrieval success is treated as useful evidence.

**Required:** Check relevance and entity/event alignment, abstain on weak matches, and
retain the eligibility reason. Also distinguish same-source/duplicate syndicated
coverage from genuinely independent corroboration: the live AP audit returned its own
article and syndicated copies among the sources.

Evidence: `audit-unverifiable.json`, `ui-ap-full-audit.txt`.

### QA-03 · P1 · Equally split evidence is resolved as Supported

**Isolated reproduction:** Actual `verify_claim` aggregation with one entailment at
0.91 and one contradiction at 0.99 returned Supported at 0.91, even with the contested
framing flag set.

**Cause:** `backend/verifier.py:258` lets entailment win when counts tie. Contrary
scores are excluded from the winning-label confidence calculation.

**Required:** Expose a conflicting/uncertain outcome, both sides and their sources;
do not decide solely by a tie-breaking count or imply calibrated certainty.

Evidence: `controlled-code-checks.json`. Retrieval and NLI were mocked for this check;
it proves aggregation behavior, not how often a real model produces that pattern.

### QA-04 · P1 · A 100/100 article score can hide an omitted false statement

**Input:** Paris is the capital of France. Paris is the capital of Germany. Apollo 11
landed humans on the Moon in July 1969.

**Observed:** Only Apollo was selected; score 100, labelled High Factual Integrity.
The false Germany sentence remained unassessed. An additional pasted-text browser
test reproduced the same presentation.

**Required:** State sampling/coverage explicitly and scope the score to assessed
claims. Avoid presenting it as whole-article factual accuracy. Pure opinion was also
forced into a fallback claim, returning Not Enough Info at 0.955 and score 50.

Evidence: `audit-mixed.json`, `audit-opinion.json`, `ui-mixed-claims-and-decimal.txt`.
Relevant code: `backend/claim_extractor.py`, `backend/scorer.py`, `CredibilityGauge.tsx`.

### QA-05 · P1 · PS 1.1 decision trace is incomplete

The API returns claim, final verdict/confidence, retrieved evidence, sources and a
rationale. It does **not** retain/return the entailment/contradiction/neutral vector
for every evidence item, nor evidence-removal influence. Only the first two passages
are evaluated (`backend/verifier.py:198`), while up to three are displayed and all
three may influence the template explanation.

**Required:** Return an evidence-by-evidence NLI ledger, evaluated/unevaluated status,
aggregation logic and a reproducible influence calculation. Presently the judge's
“which sentence changed the verdict most?” question cannot be answered from the trace.

### QA-06 · P1 · Publisher fuzzy matching assigns another outlet's rating

**Observed:** A sports story at `centredaily.com`, described as Centre Daily Times,
was labelled TIME / Lean Left. Controlled lookup returned `time.com`, name Time,
score -0.35 for Centre Daily Times.

**Cause:** The substring fallback in `backend/main.py:132` mistakes “Times” for “Time”.

**Required:** Prefer verified domains and explicit aliases. Unknown publishers must
remain unrated rather than inherit a vaguely matching publisher's identity/rating.

Evidence: `ui-sports-refreshed.txt`, `controlled-code-checks.json`.

### QA-07 · P1 · Inspector rendering can remove part of the original manuscript

**Input paragraph:** A quiet café serves coffee for $4.9 daily.

**Observed:** After an audit selected a different paragraph's Apollo claim, the
inspector showed only “9 daily.” The source editor retained the original sentence.
The same failure was observed with a $4.9 million passage in a real article.

**Cause:** Sentence regex in `ArticleReader.tsx` requires whitespace after punctuation;
when no claim matches a paragraph, a decimal point can cause a prefix to be skipped.

**Required:** Preserve every character during annotation; use offsets/tokenization
that does not discard unmatched text. Test decimals, abbreviations and punctuation.

Evidence: `ui-mixed-claims-and-decimal.txt`, `ui-source-editor-toggle.txt`.

### QA-08 · P1 · Fetch failure can still produce a successful consensus comparison

**Isolated reproduction:** Make both fetches fail. The endpoint returns two error
outlets with empty claims, default 50 credibility and Center stance, then computes
0 polarization and “Both outlets exhibit neutral or concordant coverage.”

**Cause:** `_analyze_outlet_payload` defaults (`backend/main.py:378`) are passed into
the normal divergence calculator instead of stopping comparison.

**Required:** Return an explicit unavailable/partial result with no fabricated scores
when either article cannot be analysed.

Evidence: `failure-path-results.json`, reproducible with `failure_paths.py`.

### QA-09 · P2 · Polarization descriptions exceed the metric's meaning

**Controls:** Identical texts scored zero. Paris/France versus Paris/Germany scored
6.5 and “Low Divergence (Consensus Reporting).” Keeping the Paris fact and adding
“The corrupt, incompetent regime is a disgrace” produced 83.8 and “High Polarization.”

The formula (`backend/main.py:451`) takes the maximum of political-label total
variation and blended spectrum distance. It does not measure factual overlap or
detect whether two inputs describe the same event. Keyword tone density is reported
separately; tone can also change the political classifier's output.

The AP/CBS page additionally reports low divergence but its fixed takeaway claims
divergent framing creates an echo chamber. Its “simultaneously” loading description
also differs from the sequential outlet analysis.

**Required:** Explain the existing metric narrowly; make descriptions conditional;
show supporting passages and limitations. Add factual coverage only if actually
implemented and validated. The short-text controls demonstrate possible misleading
behavior, not an estimate of performance on all news articles.

Evidence: `compare-*-result.json`, `ui-manual-ap-cbs-comparison.txt`.

### QA-10 · P2 · Wire-to-Compare is unavailable in the tested live feeds

All 125 initial feed articles lacked the two alternate perspectives required for
the card's Compare button. The current search RSS generally supplies single-story
descriptions; the parser expects list-based multi-source clusters. Therefore the
requested card-to-comparison path could not be completed from these feeds. No
synthetic perspectives were inserted to make it appear tested.

**Required:** Retrieve real same-event alternate coverage, or make the limitation
explicit and provide a working manual route. The Reuters/NBC wire pair was successfully
tested through that manual/API route instead.

Evidence: five `*-feed-checks.json`, wire UI snapshots, `compare-two-wire-articles.json`.

### QA-11 · P2 · Title-only comparison UI and API disagree

The form calls URLs optional and accepts titles in its client-side validation. A
fresh form with only two titles submits and shows “Provide an article URL or text
for both perspectives.” The backend correctly rejects insufficient article material,
but the form promises a path it does not support. Previous comparison results also
remain stored when inputs change or a later request fails (code review).

**Required:** Align the form with supported input, invalidate stale results, and make
required article content clear. Do not infer a full article from its headline.

Evidence: `ui-title-only-comparison.txt`, `compare-title-only-submit.json`.

### QA-12 · P2 · Reset and raw-text mode can retain stale article context

After resetting an article selected from the wire, an unrelated pasted Moon/Paris
audit displayed the old NYT headline. `handleAnalyze` falls back to `initialTitle`
even after local reset. Switching to paste mode alone also retains the prior URL,
which can continue to drive publisher bias for unrelated text. Returning to the
desk can reload the earlier selection held in `App`.

**Required:** Reset the parent selection/context too; distinguish editing the same
article from starting a new pasted document.

Evidence: `ui-mixed-claims-and-decimal.txt`; `Workbench.tsx:106,130,160` and `App.tsx`.

### QA-13 · P2 · XAI diagnostics are not reliably grounded decision explanations

Isolated checks found no extracted numbers for 50%/25% or $1 million/$2 million.
An unrelated town festival in 2020 caused a Not Enough Info building-opening claim
about 2000 to receive a “verified reporting” timeline-discrepancy explanation. Any
negation in aggregated evidence can similarly drive the polarity template.

**Required:** Match the same event/quantity/entity before asserting a conflict;
tie explanations to the evaluated passage and avoid unsupported “verified” wording.

Evidence: `pure-diagnostics.json`, `backend/xai_explainer.py`.

### QA-14 · P2 · Scoring and evidence-balance inconsistencies

- All-neutral evidence produces 50, labelled Mixed/Contested despite no actual conflict.
- No selected claims can produce zero, implying factual deficiencies rather than no assessment.
- The loaded-language “penalty” can raise an all-Refuted score from zero to five.
- `/analyze` reads nonexistent article-level `framing_analysis` instead of
  `loaded_language`, so its tone penalty is not applied consistently with comparison.
- The footprint says ideologically diverse/balanced when every source is Unrated.
- Spectrum cards always show 60% publisher / 40% model even when the publisher is
  unrated and the backend uses 100% model.
- The political classifier gave Left to neutral science/geography text; this is a
  domain/model limitation and should not be represented as reliable political intent.

Evidence: `pure-diagnostics.json`, `audit-punctuation.json`, raw-text UI snapshots;
`scorer.py:43`, `main.py:309`, `EchoChamberMatrix.tsx`, `SpectrumSlider.tsx`.

### QA-15 · P1 · Public URL-fetch endpoints lack network-target restrictions

Code review and model validation confirm a plain string URL accepts a loopback
target. Fetch routines follow redirects and do not restrict private/loopback/link-local
destinations. **No private endpoint or metadata service was probed.** This is a
code-confirmed exposure path, not a demonstrated theft of data.

**Required before wider sharing:** Validate schemes, DNS results and every redirect
across all fetch paths. Bound audit size/work and pending jobs. A malformed public
input already spends about six seconds attempting fallback rather than failing early.

Evidence: `failure-path-results.json`, `fetch-invalid-url.json`, `article_fetcher.py`.

## Additional maintenance/presentation findings

- Progress steps are time-based simulation and say Local CPU Engine despite CUDA.
  “Zero Generative Hallucination” is not evidence that verdicts/explanations are correct.
- Evidence snippets can display encoded HTML entities literally.
- Source lint has 12 warnings, including state updates in `useMemo` and incomplete
  effect dependencies. `npm run lint` also scans generated dist and produces much noise.
- Vite development proxy forwards `/api` to port 8000, while the client uses bare
  routes and the tested backend is port 8001. The production build works; the stated
  development arrangement needs correction before relying on it.
- Index rebuild writes `passages.pkl`, while runtime prefers existing `passages.json`.
  A rebuild could mismatch the new index and stale JSON. The live index was not rebuilt.
- `app.py` is a separate Gradio entrypoint; the tested app is `main:app`. A clean install
  of the Gradio variant, Docker image and Render deployment was not executed here.
- Print button wiring was reviewed (`window.print`); physical printing/native print
  dialogs were not exercised. Browser checks are not a cross-device/cross-browser or
  large-load certification. No destructive, denial-of-service or private-network probes
  were performed. Optional fallback providers were not exhaustively outage-tested.

## Recommended fix order

1. Preserve source identity; reject failed article ingestion/comparison; restrict public
   fetch targets. Keep the current UI and GPU runtime.
2. Correct inspector text preservation, reset metadata and publisher matching.
3. Add evidence eligibility/independence checks and explicit conflict/uncertainty handling;
   retain per-evidence NLI vectors and influence, then verify with fixed regression cases.
4. Scope scores to selected claims; correct empty/neutral/tone and balance labels.
5. Repair real wire perspective discovery, title-only form validation and comparison wording.
6. Re-run the same saved cases and verify that speed, sources and explanations remain intact.

Reference truth for the Apollo controls was checked against NASA's
[Apollo 11 mission page](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/apollo-11/).
The report's principal findings are based on the app's saved outputs and code,
not on treating a model's own response as ground truth.
